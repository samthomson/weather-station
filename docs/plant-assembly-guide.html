Breadboard first build

On a breadboard each numbered column is one node: a26–e26 are connected, a26 and a27 are not — so each signal gets its own column, and two wires of one net go into different letters of the same column. Power (3V3/GND) rides the horizontal top rails. Nothing mounts on the board: the ESP32 and probes sit beside it and reach in with jumpers.

1 · ESP32 → boardSix F–M jumpers (female ends on the ESP32's pins): 3V3 → + rail, GND → − rail, D32/D33/D34 → the d hole of columns 26 / 29 / 32, and D25 → the d hole of column 23 (probe A's switched power — step 2).
2 · Soil A dividerBare 2-wire probe, no electronics — it's just a variable resistor, so it needs a fixed partner to make a voltage: clamp wire 1 in a screw terminal with an M–M → the b hole of column 23 (D25, switched 3V3 — not the + rail: the firmware energises the probe only for its own ~5 ms measurement, so its DC can't bias probes B and C sharing the pot, and it barely electrolyses); clamp wire 2 with an M–M → the b hole of column 26. Push the 10 kΩ resistor from the a hole of column 26 into the − rail. Column 26 = the measurement node. The resistor: a small tan cylinder, bands brown·black·orange(+gold); anything 4.7 kΩ–47 kΩ works.
3 · Soil BThree F–M jumpers: VCC → +, GND → −, AOUT → the b hole of column 29.
4 · Soil CIts interface board has four pins — + F–M → + rail (orange), − F–M → − rail (blue), A0 F–M → the b hole of column 32 (green), and D0 — leave it unconnected.
5 · CheckUSB in, serial monitor at 115200: expect “[i2c] no devices found” then Soil B/C: avg=… and Soil A: avg=… (excited ~5ms) every 2 s. Any channel stuck at a constant 0 or 4095 = wrong column or unwired.

Total: 12× F–M + 2× M–M + 1× 10 kΩ + one 2-way screw terminal (Soil A's bare probe wires only). When the comparison data looks sane, rebuild it as the stripboard version below.

Stripboard matrix

One row = one net. Every module lands on the row with its own stub. All probes run on 3V3 — never 5V (the ESP32 ADC pins max out at 3.3 V, and 5 V degrades capacitive probes). DO pins stay unconnected everywhere. Soil A is the bare 2-wire HW-080 probe — no board of its own, so it rides a 10 kΩ divider: 3V3 → probe → node (Soil-A row) → 10 kΩ → GND row; its resistor leg lands in the GND row directly. GPIO 34 is input-only, perfect for analog. The three probes should end up in the same pot of soil, a few cm apart, for a like-for-like comparison.

Sensor notes

Identify-then-rename workflow: all three channels publish raw 0–4095 ADC as soil_moisture readings with editable model labels — once you trust a channel, rename it in the dashboard (Sensors → soil model fields). No reflash needed.

Soil AHW-080 bare 2-wire resistive fork — no electronics board, two wires off the probe. The probe is just a variable resistor, and the ADC reads voltage — so a fixed partner resistor is needed to turn resistance into a voltage: the 10 kΩ divider (3V3 → probe → D32 node → 10 kΩ → GND). A zero-parts alternative exists — the ESP32's internal ~45 kΩ pull-up — but its value is loosely specified and varies chip-to-chip and with temperature, which would make A's data incomparable with B and C. Keep the 10 kΩ. Wet soil = low probe resistance = node voltage rises, so this channel reads high when wet — the opposite direction of B and C. A dead-wrong channel reads a constant 0 or 4095.
Soil BCapacitive Soil Moisture Sensor v1.2 (Lazada generic, TLC555, “Corrosion Resistant v1.2” silkscreen). Header labels left-to-right: GND, VCC, AOUT. Rated 3.3–5.5 V, < 5 mA, analog out 0–3.0 V — so this channel never reaches ADC full scale; expect dry ≈ 2300–3000 raw and much lower when wet. The electronics end (top edge above the header) must stay out of the soil: the bare PCB edge wicks water — seal it with heat-shrink or wax.
Soil CGeneric OEM module (Lazada AEI.th, no model number): metal two-prong fork on a 1.3 m cable to a small interface board with four pins: + → 3V3, − → GND, A0 → D34 (analog out), D0 unconnected — D0 is the LM393 comparator's wet/dry threshold output (trimmer-set); A0 carries the full measurement, so D0 adds nothing. Firmware default label is SOIL-METAL-1M. Gotcha (found the hard way): the fork's 2-wire lead must be pushed fully into its socket on the interface board — a half-seated connector reads as bone-dry max (steady 4095) even with the fork underwater. If this channel ever pegs at 4095, reseat the fork connector before suspecting anything else.
CorrosionResistive probes (A, C) electrolyse when powered constantly — expect drift and probe wear over weeks/months. That's exactly what this comparison station is measuring. The metal C should outlast the standard A.
First bootSerial prints an I²C identification scan (expect “no devices found — all probes are analog”) and then Soil A/B/C: avg=… spread=… every 2 s. Watch the values dry in air, then dip each probe in water one at a time to map channels and wet/dry direction per model.

Cables — plant station

Three F–M parts per connection, all pluggable and no M–M anywhere: a 15 cm stub (male end soldered into the stripboard row, female socket up), a 30 cm riser (its male end into the stub's socket, female socket up), and a 10 cm sensor tail (its male end into the riser's socket, its female onto the sensor's male pin). The ESP32 skips riser and tail — its stub's female goes straight onto the DevKit's pins. Sensor C's 1.3 m pigtail already reaches the plant: one tail tier only, soldered to the pigtail.
